Sustainable Deep-Sea Extraction:
The ABAS Solution

As first-year students at IMU Kolkata, we are entering a field where traditional maritime engineering meets the extreme physics of the abyss. Deep-sea mining is the process of retrieving mineral deposits from the seafloor—specifically polymetallic nodules—to fuel the world's green energy transition.

What are we mining?

Polymetallic nodules are "rocks" roughly the size of potatoes. They form over millions of years by precipitating metals from seawater. They are rich in Nickel, Cobalt, Copper, and Manganese—the key ingredients for Electric Vehicle (EV) batteries.

"The abyss holds more critical minerals than all known terrestrial reserves combined. The challenge isn't finding them; it's retrieving them without destroying the planet."

The Engineering Choice: Problem Statement II

We have analyzed Problem Statement I (Vertical Riser) and Problem Statement II (Riser-less). For long-term sustainability, we select the Riser-less architecture.

Statement I: Vertical Riser

  • ✖ Huge hydrodynamic drag on 6km of pipe.
  • ✖ Vortex-Induced Vibrations (VIV) fatigue.
  • ✖ Energy-intensive multi-phase pumping.
  • ✖ Generates a toxic mid-water sediment plume.

Statement II: Riser-less Swarm

  • ✔ Decouples surface vessel from seafloor ops.
  • ✔ Zero mid-water dewatering plume.
  • ✔ Scalable: Redundancy across 100+ units.
  • ✔ Buoyancy-assisted travel (low energy).
